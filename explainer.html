Big picture
Whyld World helps you see hidden layers of reality. It highlights moments, stories, and context in your environment that are connected to a deeper meaning relevant only to you, giving you superpowers to see the world through new perspectives and connections.

It does that by combining five ideas:
📖 Chapters: phases of your life
🎯 Goals: what you are moving toward
⛰️ Frontiers: edges those goals sit on
🎨 Lenses: how you choose to look at things
🗺️ Life Map: the big picture over time
Chapters
Chapters are short names for the phases of your life. For example:
  • Exploring – trying things, learning, wandering
  • Building – creating something solid, investing effort
  • Healing – recovering, rebuilding capacity
  • Transforming – changing patterns or identity
  • Mastering – deepening a craft or domain
  • Leading – carrying responsibility for others
  • Living – integrating and enjoying what you have built
You choose:
  • One primary chapter you feel you are in right now
  • Up to a few sub-chapters that are active in parallel
Over time, these chapters form the spine of your Life Map.
Goals
Goals are concrete things you care about. They can be small or large:
  • Micro – this week or this month
  • Meso – this year or this current season of life
  • Macro – multi-year or life-direction goals
Each goal is connected to your story:
  • Which chapter is this goal most a part of?
  • Does it point toward a future chapter you want to grow into?
This is what lets Clara say “this action matters for the chapter you are living,” instead of treating goals as a separate to-do list.
Frontiers
Frontiers describe the kind of edge a goal sits on. They do not replace chapters. They sit on top of goals as dimensions.

For each frontier there is:
  • a barrier – the wall you feel
  • an opportunity – what becomes possible beyond it
Examples:
  • Impossibility Frontier – from “this is too big for me” to “I am growing into it.”
  • Distractibility Frontier – from scattered effort to depth and flow.
  • Isolation Frontier – from carrying things alone to shared work and support.
  • Conformity Frontier – from playing it safe to honest expression.
For a given goal, you can tag which frontiers feel relevant and, later, how far along that edge you feel.
Lenses
Lenses are ways of looking at the same moment:
  • 🧭 Explore – learning, curiosity, discovery
  • 🤝 Serve – contribution and care
  • 🎨 Create – making and expression
  • ✨ Inspire – energizing yourself or others
  • 🪞 Reflect – noticing and integrating
You choose a starting lens in Meaning Mode. Later, Clara can suggest prompts in different lenses for the same goal so you can experience it from more than one angle.
How it all fits together
A rough mental model:

Chapters describe the phase of life you are in.
Goals are the moves you care about in this phase.
Frontiers describe the edge each goal sits on.
Lenses are how you choose to look at those goals.
Life Map is the long view that ties it all into a story.
Example:
You might be in a Building chapter with sub-chapters of Transforming and Leading. One goal is “Launch my next project.”

That goal might:
  • Belong to your Building chapter
  • Point toward a future Leading chapter
  • Sit on the Impossibility and Fragmentation frontiers
  • Be approached through a Create or Explore lens
When you open Discovery Mode in a place that matters for that goal, Clara can surface prompts, stories, or “quests” that make that connection feel visible.
Where each mode fits

Meaning Mode (Landing page)

“Set the dials on your inner map.”
You choose your chapter, sub-chapters, key themes, and a first guess at your purpose. This seeds your Life Map.

Life Map Mode

“Draw the longer arc.”
You sketch past, present, and emerging chapters, turning points, and a simple constellation of goals across life areas.

Discovery Mode (AR)

“Let the world talk back.”
As you move through places, Clara overlays prompts and stories linked to your chapters, goals, and frontiers.
Quick questions
Can I be in more than one chapter at once?
Yes. You pick one chapter that feels primary, then add a few sub-chapters for other active roles or shifts.
Do I have to use frontiers?
No. Frontiers are optional layers you can turn on once you want a more detailed view of how your goals sit at different edges in your life.
Is this only about physical location?
Place is one important signal. Over time, Clara can also respond to patterns in your reflections, goals, and check-ins, so prompts feel relevant even when there is nothing special about the GPS coordinates.
